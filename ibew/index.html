XX IBEW — International Business Economics Workshop

September 1 and 2, 2026 · Universitat de les Illes Balears (UIB)

IBEW is the annual International Business Economics Workshop jointly organized around the MMOBE Master (Management, Organization and Business Economics) and the DEMO PhD programme (Doctorate in Economics, Management and Organisation) at the Universitat de les Illes Balears. Each edition combines the public defense of MMOBE master theses with a day of invited talks and work-in-progress presentations by DEMO PhD students and guests.

Massimo Riccaboni — IMT Lucca ↗

“Founding conditions, learning and the success of young firms”
with Mark Kattenberg, Fabio Incerti & Erik Stam

Using administrative data on Dutch firms founded 2006–2021 and machine learning, the paper tests noisy learning against capability learning as explanations for young-firm success. Success probabilities turn out highly persistent over a firm's life and the business cycle; differences in success by firm age mostly reflect sample selection rather than learning, and early founding conditions predict success at every stage without correlating with how that success changes over time — evidence that favors noisy learning over capability learning.

Prof. Dr. Andreas König
Chair of Strategic Management, Innovation & Entrepreneurship — University of Passau

Prof. König leads internationally recognized research on digital platform ecosystems, examining how platforms reshape value creation, power dynamics and regulation across markets. He is spokesperson of the DFG Research Training Group “Digital Platform Ecosystems”. He replaces Tim Quigley (IMD Lausanne), who is unable to attend this year.

Chair of Strategy & Innovation, University of Passau ↗

“CEO Narcissism and Business Ecosystems”
with Anastasiya Shylina, Don Lange & Mike Pfarrer

The paper theorizes how CEO narcissism shapes business ecosystems — a defining, meta-organizational phenomenon of the digital economy — through the novel concept of ecosystem oligarchization: the extent to which an ecosystem is controlled by a stratum of privileged affiliates, with the orchestrator acting as patron. Orchestrating-CEO narcissism drives oligarchization and, up to a threshold, can enhance ecosystem value co-creation; beyond that threshold, the toxic interorganizational effects of narcissism begin to undermine it.
